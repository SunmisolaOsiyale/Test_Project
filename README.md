1. Project Objective
Midlands Infrastructure Group (MIG) operates 30 construction sites across multiple
regions and has been facing challenges such as inconsistent cement consumption,
stockouts, overcapacity, and unpredictable demand patterns. These issues made it
difficult for the business to plan deliveries efficiently and prepare for accurate
forecasting.
Using the dataset provided, the Data Analyst team was responsible for examining MIG’s
current operational performance and uncovering the patterns behind these challenges.
Our goal was to transform the raw data into clear, actionable insights and build
interactive dashboards that show how each site is performing today. This work provides
MIG with a strong analytical foundation, helping the business understand its current
state and positioning it to successfully integrate and interpret future forecasting results.
2. Summary of Weeks 1 & 2
During Week One, the team focused on understanding the business problem, reviewing
the case study brief, and exploring the dataset. We cleaned and structured the data to
ensure accuracy and consistency, identified the key operational metrics, and mapped out
how each metric contributes to forecasting and performance monitoring. This foundation
ensured that the dashboards built in Week Two would be reliable and ready to receive the
forecasting output.
In Week Two, the team developed two interactive dashboards: one for executives and one
for site managers. These dashboards were designed to help MIG understand
consumption trends, operational risks, and customer behaviour patterns across regions.
We added slicers, bookmarks, and page navigation to make the dashboards easy to
explore, and we implemented Row-Level Security (RLS) so each site manager sees only
the data relevant to their site.
3. Executive Dashboard
The Executive Dashboard provides a high-level view of performance across all 30 sites
and regions. It highlights total planned pours, deliveries, and consumption, helping
leadership monitor efficiency and identify gaps. Monthly consumption trends reveal
seasonal fluctuations, while cement type distribution shows balanced usage across CEM
I, II, and III. The dashboard also identifies the top sites with pour disruption and
overcapacity, enabling executives to quickly spot operational bottlenecks and prioritize
interventions. Overall, it supports strategic decision-making and prepares leadership to
interpret and apply forecasting outputs effectively.

4. Site Manager Dashboard
The Site Manager Dashboard provides a localized view of each site’s performance, allowing
managers to understand their operational behaviour in detail. It compares planned versus actual
pours, deliveries versus consumption, and silo capacity utilization. Regional behaviour analysis
shows clear differences across East, South, West, and North, helping managers understand
how their site compares to others. Row-Level Security ensures each manager sees only their
own site, promoting accountability and focused decision-making. This dashboard equips site
managers with the insights needed to reduce waste, improve planning, and align their
operations with the forecasting model.

5. Final Insights
The dashboards revealed clear behavioural and operational patterns across regions. The
East region recorded the highest total consumption and the highest level of chaotic
behaviour, making it the most operationally demanding. The South region showed the
highest aggressive consumption and the second-highest chaotic behaviour, indicating
high volatility. The West region displayed moderate consumption with balanced
behaviour, while the North region showed no chaotic behaviour at all, making it the most
stable and predictable region.
Operationally, deliveries exceeded consumption by a significant margin, suggesting over-
delivery and potential waste. High pour disruption and overcapacity days highlighted
scheduling and silo-management inefficiencies, while low silo utilization indicated
underuse of available capacity.
6. Recommendations
To enhance operational efficiency and prepare for the forecasting model, the team
recommends the following actions:
1. 2. 3. Strengthen forecasting efforts in regions that are chaotic and aggressive.
Align deliveries more closely with actual consumption patterns.
Prioritize logistics support for high-risk sites.
4. Provide training for site managers to help them interpret behavioral patterns and
adjust ordering schedules as needed.
5. Use dashboards regularly to monitor performance trends and support
decisionmaking.
The work completed by the team ensures that MIG now has a clear, data-driven view of
cement demand and operational performance across all sites. The dashboards provide
the structure needed to integrate the forecasting model, making it easier for the client to
understand future demand and plan deliveries more effectively. This positions MIG to
reduce waste, prevent stockouts, and improve operational efficiency across the entire
network.
